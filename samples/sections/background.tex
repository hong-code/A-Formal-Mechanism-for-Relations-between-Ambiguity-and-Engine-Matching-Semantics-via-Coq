\section{Background}

\subsection{Backtracking Tree Semantics}

Barriere et al. define an inductive semantics for JavaScript regular
expressions that produces a backtracking execution trace tree rather than merely
the first match~\cite{barriere2026jsregex}.  A trace tree contains leaves for
success and failure, unary nodes for deterministic operations such as reading a
character or opening a capture group, and binary or ordered nodes for
backtracking choices.  The left-to-right order of choice nodes represents engine
priority.  The match returned by a conventional backtracking engine is the
leftmost successful leaf, while the full tree records every alternative that
would be considered by a complete search.

For this paper, we abstract this idea to a regular core.  Let
\(\trace(r,w)\) be the ordered trace tree for regular expression \(r\) and word
\(w\).  We write \(\mathsf{SuccLeaves}(\trace(r,w))\) for the list of successful
leaves, preserving priority order.  The returned engine match, when it exists,
is the head of this list.

\subsection{Automata Ambiguity}

Let an NFA be \(\nfa=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,I,F)\).  A run over
\(w=a_1\cdots a_n\) is a path
\[
  q_0 \xrightarrow{a_1} q_1 \xrightarrow{a_2} \cdots
  \xrightarrow{a_n} q_n
\]
with \(q_0\in I\).  It is accepting when \(q_n\in F\).  The ambiguity of
\(\nfa\) on \(w\), written \(\amb_{\nfa}(w)\), is the number of accepting runs
of \(\nfa\) on \(w\).  The degree of ambiguity of \(\nfa\) is
\[
  \sup_{w\in\Sigma^*} \amb_{\nfa}(w).
\]
An automaton is unambiguous when this degree is at most one, finitely ambiguous
when the supremum is finite, polynomially ambiguous when \(\amb_{\nfa}(w)\) is
bounded by a polynomial in \(|w|\), and exponentially ambiguous when no
polynomial bound exists.

Weber and Seidl give structural criteria and bounds for these classes.  In
particular, infinite ambiguity is characterized by a simple pattern in the
automaton and is decidable in polynomial time; the degree of polynomial
ambiguity growth is also computable in polynomial time~\cite{weber1991ambiguity}.
These criteria are exactly the kind of finite certificate that a proof assistant
can check once the underlying graph theory has been formalized.

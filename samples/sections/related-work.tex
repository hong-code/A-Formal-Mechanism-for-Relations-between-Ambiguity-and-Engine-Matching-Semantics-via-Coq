\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Algebraic Semantics of Regular Languages}

The algebraic treatment of regular languages is rooted in the classical
correspondence between regular expressions, finite automata, and regular
events. In Kleene algebra, union, concatenation, the empty language, and
the singleton language $\{\varepsilon\}$ provide the underlying
idempotent semiring structure, while Kleene star accounts for finite
iteration \cite{Kozen1994}. More general semiring-based frameworks have
also played a central role in automata theory, weighted automata,
rational series, and rational subsets
\cite{KuichSalomaa1986,BerstelReutenauer2011}. These works make clear
that regularity is not a property of an arbitrary powerset carrier
itself, but of the rational elements generated from basic languages by
the regular operations. This algebraic viewpoint provides the standard
background for introducing new language domains equipped with union,
concatenation, identity, zero, and star operations.

\subsection{Derivatives and Automata Constructions}

Derivatives give a residual account of regular-expression matching.
Brzozowski derivatives describe the language remaining after a prefix
has been consumed and lead naturally to deterministic automata
\cite{brzozowski1964derivatives}. Antimirov partial derivatives refine
this idea by collecting the possible residual branches as a finite set
of expressions, yielding nondeterministic partial-derivative automata
\cite{antimirov1996partial}. This construction has also been formalized
in Coq \cite{almeida2011partial}. In parallel, position automata,
introduced independently by Glushkov and by McNaughton and Yamada,
construct states from marked symbol occurrences
\cite{glushkov1961abstract,mcnaughton1960regular}. Follow automata and
equation automata further reduce or reorganize position-based
constructions by quotienting future behaviours, and recent work studies
their relationships through quotient constructions, state equivalences,
and isomorphisms \cite{lu2025relations}. Together, these lines of work
show two complementary ways of retaining syntactic information:
residual expressions as automaton states, and marked occurrences as
structural indices.

\subsection{Regular Expressions with Lookahead}

Lookahead extends regular expressions with zero-width assertions whose
truth is evaluated at intermediate input positions rather than by
ordinary consumption. Earlier work by Morihata studied the translation
of regular expressions with lookahead into finite automata, showing that
lookahead does not by itself destroy regularity
\cite{morihata2012translation}. Miyazaki and Minamide later developed a
derivative-based treatment of regular expressions with lookahead, where
residual behaviours are used to construct deterministic automata
\cite{miyazaki2019derivatives}. Berglund, van der Merwe, and van
Litsenborgh translated lookahead expressions into Boolean automata and
studied the associated state complexity \cite{berglund2021lookahead}.
Mamouras and Chattopadhyay proposed efficient matching algorithms for
regular expressions with lookaround assertions
\cite{mamouras2024efficient}. On the logical side, Nakamura gave a
PDL-style axiomatization for regular expressions with lookahead and
studied both match-language equivalence and ordinary language
equivalence \cite{nakamura2026complete}. These works emphasize that
lookahead requires semantics sensitive to matching boundaries and
right-context information, beyond ordinary whole-input language
equivalence.

\subsection{Mechanized Verification of Regular Expressions}

Mechanized verification of regular expressions has developed along
several lines, including verified automata constructions, algebraic
decision procedures, and faithful semantics for practical regex
languages. Almeida et al. formalized partial-derivative automata in Coq
\cite{almeida2011partial}, while Braibant and Pous developed a Coq
decision procedure for Kleene algebra based on verified automata and
algebraic reasoning \cite{braibant2012}. Doczkal and Smolka gave a
constructive Coq development of regular-language representations,
including automata, regular expressions, and translations between them
\cite{doczkal2018regular}. More recent work has extended mechanized
reasoning to richer regex features and realistic semantics: Zhuchko,
Veanes, and Ebner formalized extended regular-expression matching with
lookarounds in Lean \cite{zhuchko2024}; Chattopadhyay, Li, and Mamouras
verified an efficient lookaround matcher in Coq
\cite{chattopadhyay2025verified}; De Santo, Barri{\`e}re, and
Pit-Claudel mechanized JavaScript regular-expression semantics in Coq
\cite{desanto2024}; and Barri{\`e}re, Deng, and Pit-Claudel later
developed a Rocq mechanization of JavaScript regex semantics and its
applications \cite{barriere2026}. These developments provide the
formal-verification background for machine-checked reasoning about
regex semantics, derivative constructions, automata correctness, and lookaround features.
